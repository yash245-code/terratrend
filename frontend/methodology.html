Orbital Earth Intelligence Meets Sovereign Farm Economics.

TerraTrend synthesizes 15 to 20 years of continuous NASA satellite soundings and reanalysis cubes with the official cost-accounting standards of the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP), Government of India. This document details the underlying physical data sources, regional topography calibrations, mathematical formulas, and statistical gates powering the platform.

03 Statistical Trend Detection: Hamed-Rao Modified Mann-Kendall

In classical climate analysis, standard linear regression is vulnerable to extreme cloud outliers, sporadic drought spikes, and sensor recalibrations. Standard Mann-Kendall tests assume statistically independent observations. In real-world Indian meteorological records, positive serial autocorrelation (seasonal persistence) inflates the test score, causing false-positive trend alerts in up to 40% of stationary series.

TerraTrend implements the Hamed and Rao (1998) Modified Mann-Kendall test, which detects true monotonic shifts while filtering out seasonal cyclical noise:

Autocorrelation-Corrected Rank Testing

The Mann-Kendall test systematically compares every chronological pair of observations across the 10-year satellite record to determine whether consecutive years consistently increase or decrease in rank. Rather than relying on simple linear trends that can be skewed by a single severe flood or drought year, it evaluates the monotonic consistency of the entire multi-year trajectory.

Variance Adjustment for Meteorological Memory

Hydro-meteorological records exhibit serial persistence (multi-month or multi-year memory). The Hamed-Rao correction calculates the empirical autocorrelation structure and adjusts the test variance across all statistically significant lags. This eliminates false alarms and ensures that multi-year cyclical dry spells are not mistaken for permanent land degradation.

Theil-Sen Robust Median Slope Estimator

The rate of environmental change per decade is determined using the non-parametric Theil-Sen estimator:

Robust Median Annual Slope

The rate of change per decade is calculated as the median of all pairwise slopes across the observation series. With an asymptotic breakdown point of nearly 30%, extreme outlier years (such as unseasonal cyclones or brief satellite transmission gaps) cannot distort the underlying multi-decadal trajectory.

Strict Statistical Significance Gate (95% Confidence)

TerraTrend enforces a strict conservative threshold: unless the two-tailed probability demonstrates a 95% statistical significance level (p-value under 0.05), the system classifies the metric as STABLE. Trend-based adaptation advice never recommends changing crop rotations based on unconfirmed short-term noise.

04 5-Factor Agronomic Suitability & The Weakest-Link Principle

Traditional land evaluation platforms frequently use binary thresholds (e.g. "Wheat requires under 25°C; 25.1°C returns 0%"). This introduces artificial decision cliffs. TerraTrend evaluates land through continuous 4-knot piecewise curves across five agronomic foundations:

Continuous 4-Knot Agronomic Suitability Curves

  • Lethal Minimum & Maximum: Environmental boundaries below or above which crop failure occurs (Score: 0/100).
  • Marginal Tolerated Range: Suboptimal conditions where crops experience physiological stress but survive with reduced yields (Score: 1 to 99/100).
  • Optimal Plateau: The agronomic sweet spot where temperature, rainfall, or soil chemical factors maximize productive potential (Score: 100/100).

The Five Foundations

  • 1. Temperature (Weight 20%): Evaluates growing-season heat accumulation, frost vulnerability, and extreme thermal stress.
  • 2. Moisture & Water (Weight 25%): Evaluates annual rainfall volume against crop requirement, root-zone wetness (0–100cm), and irrigation access.
  • 3. Soil pH (Weight 15%): Evaluates topsoil (0–30cm) and subsoil (30–100cm) active acidity/alkalinity; flags nutrient lockup when pH < 5.5 or sodicity when pH > 8.5.
  • 4. Soil Texture & Quality (Weight 25%): Evaluates organic carbon (%), cation exchange capacity (CEC), USDA textural class, drainage permeability, and salinity (ECe).
  • 5. Macro Climate & Stability (Weight 15%): Evaluates rainfall interannual coefficient of variation (CV), length of growing period (LGP in months), and drought frequency.

Liebig's Law of the Minimum (Poor Factor Capping)

Crop physiology is limited by the scarcest essential resource, not by the total sum of available resources. TerraTrend enforces a strict rule: If any single factor is rated POOR (below 70), the composite score cannot exceed 69.0, and the limiting factors are explicitly named on the report card.

05 Official CACP Agricultural Economics Framework

TerraTrend's economic engine adheres strictly to the cost concepts defined by the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP), Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare:

CACP Cost Concept Constituent Expenditures Institutional Significance
Cost A2 (Direct Paid-Out Working Capital) Purchased seed, chemical fertilizers, manure, plant protection chemicals, diesel/electricity, tractor hire, irrigation cess, hired human labour, animal draft power. Actual out-of-pocket operational cash required by the cultivator before planting season.
Cost A2 + FL (Paid-Out Plus Family Labor) Cost A2 plus the imputed economic value of unpaid family labour based on statutory agricultural wage rates. The statutory floor benchmark utilized by the Government of India for setting Minimum Support Prices (MSP set at 1.5 times the Cost A2 + FL baseline).
Cost C2 (Comprehensive Economic Cost) Cost A2 + FL plus rental value of owned land, rent paid for leased land, interest on owned fixed capital, and a 10% managerial allowance. The complete economic cost of production recommended by the National Commission on Farmers (Dr. M.S. Swaminathan Committee).

Financial Viability & Risk Buffer Metrics

The platform translates physical crop models into clear farm viability indicators:

  • Gross Revenue: Calculated as harvest yield multiplied by farm acreage and realized mandi market price, factoring in standardized ICAR post-harvest loss allowances (typically 4.5% to 8.0% for field handling, threshing, storage, and transit).
  • Break-Even Yield & Price: The minimum physical yield (in quintals per hectare) or market price required to completely cover comprehensive cultivation expenditures under C2 accounting.
  • Margin of Safety: The percentage cushion that market prices or farm yields can drop before the enterprise slips into negative cash flow, serving as an institutional credit safety gauge.

06 5,000-Draw Monte Carlo Downside Risk Simulation

Farm financial outcomes in India are subject to compound monsoon shocks and harvest price volatility. TerraTrend runs an automated 5,000-draw lognormal Monte Carlo risk simulation:

  • Yield Shocks: Sampled from a lognormal distribution parameterized by state-level historical yield standard deviations from the Directorate of Economics & Statistics (DES).
  • Price Volatility: Modeled using Agmarknet wholesale mandi price variances, bounded below by the statutory Minimum Support Price (MSP) floor.
  • Percentile Distribution: Reports P10 (conservative downside / drought year), P50 (median baseline), and P90 (favorable monsoon upside) net returns.
  • Probability of Loss: The empirical percentage of simulated iterations where Gross Revenue fails to cover total C2 cost of cultivation.
